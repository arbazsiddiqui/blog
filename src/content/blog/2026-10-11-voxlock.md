[VoxLock](https://huggingface.co/arbazsiddiqui/voxlock) keeps the caller's voice and removes the other voices in the room before speech-to-text hears them. It is made for voice agents: one input, the call audio, and it picks out the caller by itself.

The same 6.5 seconds of a held-out call, with VoxLock off and then on.

It has the lowest word error rate on the Krisp Voice Isolation Benchmark of every model tested, paid or open: 4.95%, with 0.11 background words leaked per minute. Below, it runs in your browser.

## How it works

VoxLock is a causal band-split RNN target-speaker extractor, 27M parameters, fine-tuned from the [REAL-TSE challenge baseline](https://github.com/REAL-TSE/wesep-real-tse). It takes the loudest 3 seconds in the first 15 seconds of the call as the caller's voice sample. The sample becomes frame-level speaker features that each frequency band attends to, and the network predicts a mask over the call's spectrogram that keeps the matching voice.

It streams with 24 ms of algorithmic latency, plus the chunk. With the default 8-bit model on one CPU thread of a Google Cloud Intel Xeon, a live call takes 77% of a core with 40 ms chunks and 61% with 80 ms chunks, so one vCPU runs one call. On an NVIDIA L4 GPU, a whole recording runs 40 times faster than real time.

## Training

Four stages on a single NVIDIA L4, 9,200 steps in total:

1. **Simulated calls:** LibriSpeech callers mixed with one or two background talkers in simulated rooms, a quarter of them telephone band, with the voice sample cut from the mix.
2. **Real crosstalk:** AMI headset recordings from 40 meetings, where the other people in the room bleed into the wearer's microphone.
3. **Long calls:** 30-second clips where the caller talks throughout.
4. **Rooms and distances:** callers close to or far from the microphone, background voices dry or in the room, and real room noise.

## Use it

```bash
pip install voxlock
voxlock call.wav -o caller.wav
```

```python
import voxlock

vl = voxlock.VoxLock(threads=1)            # downloads the 74 MB model on first use
caller = vl.isolate(voxlock.load_audio("call.wav"))

stream = vl.live()                         # live: finds the caller by itself
for chunk in call_audio:                   # e.g. 40 ms = 640 samples
    send(stream.push(chunk))               # the isolated audio, about 24 ms behind
send(stream.flush())                       # at the end of the call
```

Live, VoxLock passes the audio through until it has heard 3 seconds of the caller, then isolates. At the switch it first runs the model over the call so far, so it never starts cold in the middle of a sentence. On the 265 Krisp recordings `live()` scores 5.15% word error rate, against 4.95% on the whole file. With a sample saved from an earlier call, `vl.stream(sample)` isolates from the first chunk.

For voice agents built on Pipecat, it is an audio filter that takes 61% of one CPU core: `pip install "voxlock[pipecat]"`. It is also built into the speech denoisers of [sherpa-onnx](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx) from version 2.0.1, in C++ with no Python.

pip runs an 8-bit version of the model by default: 5.11% word error rate on the same benchmark, still ahead of every other model above, with a third less CPU than the float32 model. The model is on Hugging Face as safetensors for PyTorch and as ONNX in float32, float16 and 8-bit. The repository has the PyTorch inference, the ONNX export, the benchmark harness and the training code. Apache-2.0.
